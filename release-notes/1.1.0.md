**Highlight text**
- Select some words and pick a colour (yellow, green, blue or pink) in the toolbar that appears, or press ⌘⇧H for yellow. Choose the same colour again to take it off, or another to change it. Highlights are saved with the note, shown in exports, and written as `==text==` in the plain-text form.

**Move pictures and equations**
- Pick up a picture or a display equation and drop it anywhere in the note: a bar shows where it will land. For a picture, the side of the note decides where it sits: drop it at the left or right edge and the text wraps around it, or in the middle to centre it. A picture inside a sentence lifts out into a line of its own. Esc cancels the move.

**Pictures hold their ground**
- Bullets and numbers beside a picture are no longer hidden under it, and equations, quotes and code blocks sit beside a picture without running under it.
- Headings and tables always start below a picture. A blank line ends the text beside a picture: anything after it starts below both the picture and its text, even when the text beside it is short.

**Equations and the cursor**
- Clicking a display equation selects it and no longer adds an empty line or bullet. From there the arrow keys carry on past it, Enter opens a new line beneath it, typing starts a line beneath it, and Backspace or Delete removes it.
- Arrowing onto an equation selects it first, and equations that sit one after another are stepped through one at a time, without blank lines appearing between them.
- Backspace at the start of a bullet that follows a bullet ending in an equation now selects the equation first (a second press removes it) instead of silently merging the bullets and losing the equation.
- Deleting an equation leaves the caret in the line you were working on.

**Scheduled notes with your own titles**
- Set a subject's regular times right on its page, in the new Schedule section (for example, CoStaff meetings on your NROTC page). Each item can have a note title such as `{date} - CoStaff Meeting` (`{date}` is "Fri, Nov 6", `{iso}` is 2026-11-06, and `{weekday}` and `{subject}` work too). When the time comes, Notival offers to open that day's note, made with that title the first time. Turn off "Class timer" on an item to just open the note without starting class mode.

**Language subjects: vocab and listening practice**
- Make any subject a language subject when you create it (naming it after a language, like "Russian", picks the language for you), or later from Edit subject or Add → Customize subject. Pick the language (Russian, Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Ukrainian, Polish, Japanese, Mandarin, Korean or Arabic). Its page then has a **Vocab** section and a **Listening** tab, and each of its notes has a **Vocab** drawer. Other subjects are unchanged.
- **Vocab:** add a word and its meaning, or select words in a note and choose "Add to vocab" (⌘⇧V opens the drawer, and again hides it). Search, click a word to edit it, and press Quiz to test yourself on the meanings. Words stay with their subject.
- **Type Russian on any keyboard** (Russian subjects): "privet" becomes "привет" as you type (sh ш, ch ч, zh ж, shch щ, kh х, ya я, yu ю, yo ё, eh э, y ы, j й; the apostrophe key ' is the soft sign ь, as in sem' → семь, and a double quote " is the hard sign ъ). A ? button beside the toggle shows the keys.
- **Listening** is a tab on the subject's page, outside any note: add a voice message (an iMessage voice message, a Voice Memo, an mp4 or m4a, and most other audio files). Play it with replay-last-3-seconds, 0.75× and 0.5× speeds, and an A–B repeat for a part you want again and again. Write what you hear, then your English, then check: every word is marked right, close (wrong ending), wrong or missed, with a score and a history of attempts. Tap a word to add it to your vocab, or add everything you missed at once. To keep writing while you listen, pressing play or pause keeps the cursor in the box you are typing in, Esc plays and pauses without leaving it, pausing backs up a couple of seconds (you choose how far), and "Pause while I type" stops the audio when you start writing and carries on a moment after you stop.
- The Mac drafts the words so there is something to check against. It does this on the Mac itself; if your Mac doesn't have offline dictation for that language, Notival says so and only uses Apple's servers if you choose "Use Apple's servers this once". Adding the correct text yourself replaces the draft. If a draft looks too short for the audio, Notival says so and offers to try again.

**Show only what you use**
- Notival has a lot in it, so you can now hide what you don't need. Click the sliders button in the top bar (beside Appearance), or press ⌘,. Pick a starting point (Everything, Simple, or Just writing) or flip individual switches for sidebar items, top-bar buttons, the buttons above a note, the formatting bar, subject tabs and the sections of a subject's page. Nothing is deleted; every switch brings its part straight back, and this panel itself is never hidden.

**Push boxes out of the way**
- The boxes on the right of a subject page (Recall, Schedule, To do, Upcoming dates…) and the To do box on Home each have a small tab on their right edge. Click it and the box slides off to the right; a labelled tab stays at the edge of the window to bring it back, and the rest of the page closes up around the gap. Which boxes are pushed aside is remembered. The "Everything" preset in Show and hide brings them all back.
- Study stays in the sidebar whatever you hide, so Home is never alone.

**Smarter reviews**
- Recall now schedules with a real memory model (FSRS) instead of fixed multipliers. It keeps track of how stable and how difficult each question is for you, and brings it back just before you'd forget it. Your existing reviews carry over.
- It learns you: it compares what it predicted with what actually happened and stretches or shortens your intervals to match; a right answer that took far longer than usual is suggested as Hard; and a question that keeps slipping gets a banner suggesting you reword it or set it aside.
- It knows your tests: with a test or deadline coming up, questions for that subject come back sooner so nothing fades right before it.
- A quick "How are you feeling today?" (Fresh, OK, Tired) sizes the session: a tired day is a lighter one with no new questions, and a big backlog or shaky recent recall slows new questions down. The session also shows about how long it will take.
- A Memory section in Recall shows how much you've been recalling lately, the week of reviews ahead, when you remember best, and (with a test coming up) how much you'd remember on test day if you stopped now, with a button to review the shaky ones.
- Vocab joins Recall: each word comes back at the right moment, first as "what does it mean?", once that has stuck as "how do you say it?" (typed with the Latin keyboard for Russian), and once that has stuck as its example sentence with the word left out. The **Review** button in a language subject's Vocab section shows how many words are waiting and opens Recall for that subject. Add an example sentence to a word (Listening fills it in for words you pick up from a message), and a backtick after a vowel adds a stress mark (вода` → вода́).
- **Practise** in a language subject's Vocab section is free word practice: you type the answers and choose the direction (the word → its meaning, the meaning → the word, both mixed together, or **Listen and type**, where the Mac says the word aloud in the language's voice and you type what you heard), which words (a smart mix of what's due and new, shakiest first, newest first, or shuffled) and how many. A meaning can be given several ways ("hello; hi", "to eat" or "eat"). Every answer still feeds the memory model, and a speaker button on each word lets you hear it.
- Stress marks and ё don't count against a typed answer, and й and и stay different letters in Listening.

**Memory palaces**
- Every subject has a **Memory palace** tab. Build a house (start from a furnished starter house, or from an empty plot), then walk through it in 3D and leave what you want to remember on the furniture. Make one that looks like somewhere you know: it works best with a place you already picture clearly.
- **Plan view** is where you build. Drag out rooms and resize them by their edges, click a wall to add or remove a door, window or archway, and add furniture from a catalogue of about 30 pieces by clicking it or dragging it onto the plan. Move pieces by dragging, turn them with R, swap one piece for another, change colours, and undo anything with ⌘Z. Furniture sits flush against walls and can't overlap; paintings, mirrors and clocks hang on walls.
- **Walk view** puts you inside: drag to look, W A S D or the arrow keys to walk (Shift to hurry), click the floor to go there. Walls and furniture stop you.
- **Things to remember:** select a piece and leave something on it: write it yourself, or take it from the subject's vocab or questions, with an optional vivid picture to tie it to the spot. **Fill the house** places a whole list around the house in the order you would walk past it, starting at the front door.
- **Recall walk:** the camera takes you stop by stop; picture what you left, then check. Each walk is recorded.

**Fixed**
- Home and Study in the sidebar are as tall as New note.
- The checkmark in the dates and checklist circles sits in the middle of the circle.

## Memory palaces look like homes

- Walls, floors and fabrics now have real textures (wood boards, tile grout, carpet, paint), trim, curtains, doors with handles, soft shadows and a garden outside the windows.

## Objects in your palace

- Put small things on furniture: books on shelves, a mug or laptop on a desk, a vase on the table, a candle, a globe, a photo frame and more. Click one in the Build tab with a piece of furniture selected, or drag it onto the furniture.
- Give any object a meaning and make it a stop on your recall walk.
- In the walk-through, click a book and it comes off the shelf in front of you; turn its pages by clicking, the arrow keys or the buttons.
- Fill a book, notebook or letter with pages from one of your notes, your vocab, or your questions, or write the pages yourself.
- Bookshelves now have real shelves and the starter house has books to take down, starting with a short guide to how a palace works.

## Build while you walk, and more floors

- Walking view now has Explore and Build. In Build, pick any piece or small thing in the panel, point where it should go (a green ghost shows the spot) and click. Select, move, turn and delete things from inside the house too.
- Books and other things light up and show a hint when you point at them.
- Dragging to look now grabs the view: the spot under the pointer stays under it.
- Add floors with + Floor in the top bar. A new floor copies the outline of the one below. Put a Staircase on one floor and it makes the matching stairwell above; click the stairs in the walk-through to climb.
- You can leave things to remember on any floor, and the recall walk climbs the stairs for you.
