**Procedure boxes, easier to write**
- Edit a procedure right where it sits in your note: the steps on top, the typeset result live underneath as you type. Click away or press ⌘↵ to keep it, Esc to undo your changes.
- Less LaTeX typing: subscripts and powers like `x_i`, `p_{LM}`, `x^2`, `10^9` and `log_2 n` are typeset without dollar signs, just like `\in` and `\Sigma` already were. Ordinary words such as `max_len` stay as written.
- A new procedure starts blank, and long steps wrap instead of running off the edge.

**Materials in your order**
- Put a subject's materials in the order you like — say, the order the lectures were taught. Drag a row by its ⋮⋮ handle, or click the handle and press ↑ ↓. Each row shows its number in the list.

**Writing**
- Tab indents a line of text, one step per press; Shift-Tab (or Backspace at the start of the line) brings it back. Select several lines to indent them together. In a code block Tab types spaces, and in bullets it nests as before.

**Fixed**
- A flag stays on exactly the words you flagged. Pressing Enter after a flagged bullet no longer flags the next bullet, and typing right before or after a flag stays outside it.
- Enter on an empty bullet always ends the list, even when the bullet still holds something you can't see, and Backspace on an empty bullet turns it into a plain line instead of jumping back to the bullet above.
- A numbered list keeps counting past a display equation. Put an equation under step 1 and the next line is 2, whether you write the equation on the next bullet or below the list and then type "2.". A list can also start at any number, such as "3.".
- Clicking Notival in the Dock while it's already open just brings the window back, without replaying the opening animation. The animation now plays only when the app starts.
- Deleting a bullet right under a display equation no longer deletes the equation too. Backspace at the start of that bullet turns it into a plain line; Delete at the end of a bullet just above an equation selects the equation instead of removing it.
- From a line directly under an equation, the first Backspace now selects the equation and a second one removes it, so an equation never disappears from a single keystroke meant for the line below.
