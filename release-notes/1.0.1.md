**Procedure boxes, easier to write**
- Edit a procedure right where it sits in your note: the steps on top, the typeset result live underneath as you type. Click away or press ⌘↵ to keep it, Esc to undo your changes.
- Less LaTeX typing: subscripts and powers like `x_i`, `p_{LM}`, `x^2`, `10^9` and `log_2 n` are typeset without dollar signs, just like `\in` and `\Sigma` already were. Ordinary words such as `max_len` stay as written.
- A new procedure starts blank, and long steps wrap instead of running off the edge.

**Materials in your order**
- Put a subject's materials in the order you like — say, the order the lectures were taught. Drag a row by its ⋮⋮ handle, or click the handle and press ↑ ↓. Each row shows its number in the list.

**Write around your notes: group lines with a brace**
- Hover a line and a small } appears in the right margin. Drag it down over the lines that belong together (or click it for one line), type a note beside the brace, and press Enter to carry on writing where you were. You can also select across lines and press ⌘} or choose "Group }".
- Side notes: click in the blank margin beside a paragraph (or press ⌘') and type a short note in a little bubble, joined to its line by a faint lead line. Drag it anywhere; it stays with the line nearest it.
- Draw a shape to write in: hold ⌘ for a moment and the page is ready to draw on. Sketch a rough square, oval, diamond or triangle anywhere and it becomes a clean, straight-sided one (a near-square becomes a true square, a near-circle a true circle) to write in, with $…$ for an equation. It grows as you write. Let go of ⌘ to stop drawing; ⌘ with any other key is still a shortcut. A loop dragged across blank page works too, without the key.
- "Mark up" in the format bar lists these, and "Draw a shape" there (or ⌘⇧B) lets you draw one without holding a key. The } for starting a brace is bigger and easier to grab.
- The ⋯ on any drawing changes its shape and sets the outline colour and the text colour, separately.
- Exported PDFs include your drawings: shapes, side notes and boxes sit beside the lines they belong to, in their colours and with equations typeset, and a brace runs down the right of its lines with its note beside it. A Word export keeps each note as a short aside under its line.
- All of them stay with their lines when you add text above, when lines wrap, when the window changes size, and when you reopen the note. Click one to edit it, and ⌘Z undoes changes. When the window is too narrow, they fold into small badges beside their lines that you can click to read.
- Text boxes no longer change colour as the pointer passes over them.

**Link words to an equation or line**
- Select some words, choose "Link to…" in the toolbar that appears (or press ⌘⇧L), then click the equation or line they refer to. The words become a link: hover them to preview what they point to (equations shown typeset), click them to go there, and use "↩ Back" to return. ⌥-click puts the caret in the words to edit them.
- The line pointed at gets a small ↩ in the margin listing where it's referred to from, with Unlink. Links stay in the note and never appear on the mind map.

**Writing**
- Tab indents a line of text, one step per press; Shift-Tab (or Backspace at the start of the line) brings it back. Select several lines to indent them together. In a code block Tab types spaces, and in bullets it nests as before.

**Fixed**
- Unit cards show just the name you gave the unit, without an automatic "Unit 1, 2, …" label above it that could disagree with your own numbering.
- A flag stays on exactly the words you flagged. Pressing Enter after a flagged bullet no longer flags the next bullet, and typing right before or after a flag stays outside it.
- Enter on an empty bullet always ends the list, even when the bullet still holds something you can't see, and Backspace on an empty bullet turns it into a plain line instead of jumping back to the bullet above.
- A numbered list keeps counting past a display equation. Put an equation under step 1 and the next line is 2, whether you write the equation on the next bullet or below the list and then type "2.". A list can also start at any number, such as "3.".
- Clicking Notival in the Dock while it's already open just brings the window back, without replaying the opening animation. The animation now plays only when the app starts.
- Deleting a bullet right under a display equation no longer deletes the equation too. Backspace at the start of that bullet turns it into a plain line; Delete at the end of a bullet just above an equation selects the equation instead of removing it.
- From a line directly under an equation, the first Backspace now selects the equation and a second one removes it, so an equation never disappears from a single keystroke meant for the line below.
